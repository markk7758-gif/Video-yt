I'm the only bear who ever joined an army. Private Wojtek, Polish Second Corps.

It's 1942, in Iran. Polish soldiers, just out of Soviet prison camps, buy an orphan bear cub from a local boy. That's me.

They raise me on condensed milk, from a vodka bottle.

Soon I drink beer. I eat cigarettes. I wrestle soldiers, and I win.

Then, a problem. The army is shipping out to Italy, and the ship won't take pets.

So they enlist me. Name, rank, serial number. I'm a soldier now.

1944. Monte Cassino, one of the bloodiest battles of the war. The men carry heavy crates of shells up to the guns. And, the soldiers swear, so did I.

Wait. Why would a bear do that?

You see, a bear raised by people copies them. Like a puppy that thinks it's human, I thought I was one of the guys.

So they made me their badge: a bear carrying a shell. Then they promoted me. Corporal Wojtek.

Luckily, the war ends. Unluckily, my friends can't go home. Poland isn't free.

Neither am I. I spend sixteen years in Edinburgh Zoo.

Old Polish soldiers still visit. They shout to me in Polish.

And I always look up. I'm the only one there who understands them.
