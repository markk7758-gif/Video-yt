## Description
In 1942, Polish soldiers just freed from Soviet prison camps bought an orphan bear cub in Iran. They named him Wojtek.

He drank beer, ate cigarettes, wrestled his friends and, when the ship to Italy wouldn't take pets, he was enlisted as a real soldier. At Monte Cassino, the soldiers swore he carried crates of shells. He was promoted to corporal and became his company's badge.

After the war, his friends couldn't go home. Neither could he. He spent his last 16 years in Edinburgh Zoo, where old Polish veterans came to talk to him in Polish.

Sources: Imperial War Museum; BBC News; Aileen Orr, "Wojtek the Bear: Polish War Hero" (2010); Royal Zoological Society of Scotland.
