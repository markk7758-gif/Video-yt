Imagine fighting a war, and on Christmas Eve, your enemy starts singing.

It's December 1914. You're in a frozen, muddy trench in Belgium. The enemy is so close you can hear them cough. The Pope begged both sides to stop for Christmas. Both said no.

Then, that night, little lights appear on the German side. Christmas trees, glowing with candles. Someone starts singing "Silent Night." You know that tune. So your side sings back.

Next morning, a German soldier climbs out. No rifle. He just waves. Nobody shoots.

Soon, thousands of men are standing in no man's land, shaking hands. They swap cigarettes, chocolate, even buttons off their coats. They bury their dead, side by side. Some letters home say they even played football.

Wait. How does a song stop a war?

You see, the men in both trenches were the same. Cold, scared, homesick, singing the same carol in two languages. It's hard to shoot someone you just heard sing.

The generals hated it. They banned it, and the next Christmas, the guns kept firing.

The war went on for four more years. But for one night, about a hundred thousand men just... stopped.
